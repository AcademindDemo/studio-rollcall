# Critique of Grok’s payment and waitlist research

Grok’s research usefully connects vendor behavior to desk work, but its blanket “Confidence is high except where noted” is difficult to audit: there are no source links, article titles, or citations attached to most claims. Capabilities also need qualification by booking channel, configuration, and subscription tier. Neither vendor feature availability nor a plausible operational story establishes what small studios usually do.

## Vendor claims needing correction or qualification

In §2, “Arketa, low-to-medium” treats automatic class promotion as marketing and relies on appointment documentation for email claiming. That understates the available evidence. Arketa’s [class waitlist overview](https://help.arketa.com/schedule/waitlist/overview) explicitly documents automatic movement and charging, and an alternative where clients receive email and claim before someone else does. My file’s distinction between automatic promotion and nonexclusive manual claiming is better supported. Neither establishes an exclusive sequential offer.

The §1 statement that Mindbody permits unpaid online signups needs a channel caveat. Its [app and web overview](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/MINDBODY-consumer-website?language=en_US) says the Mindbody web app does not support unpaid online signups. Configurable unpaid reservations elsewhere do not establish that every online channel permits them. My file flags this restriction; Grok’s broader wording could mislead a comparison of member booking experiences.

“Glofox auto-book needs Pay Later on” (§2) needs a precise source and scope. Glofox’s [payment-processing guide](https://support.glofox.com/hc/en-us/articles/46466538745236-Getting-Started-with-Payment-Processing) verifies Pay Later booking and arrival collection, but does not establish that prepaid-credit waitlist promotion requires that setting. My file describes credit/card settlement from developer documentation; however, that linked booking-flow page now redirects to a portal, so my claim also needs a recoverable source before resolving this disagreement.

Walla’s exact refund timing, failed-payment treatment, and 24-hour fee-waiver detail are presented confidently without inspectable citations. They may be accurate, but should remain unverified here. “Has no offer-and-wait mode in the docs” (§2) is appropriately narrower than claiming the product lacks the feature; that distinction should be preserved.

## Operational reasoning that overreaches

“An automatic booking inside two hours cannot be dropped for free” (§2) imports a penalty policy. VISION.md permits cancellation up to two hours before class; it specifies neither late-cancel charges nor credit forfeiture. My file’s insistence on a human-defined rule is stronger. Grok’s question about whether late cancellation should cost a credit is useful, but conflicts with treating that consequence as settled earlier.

“Pay-at-the-door is the smaller build” (§1) is not established. With packs and memberships, door settlement still needs entitlement checks, reconciliation, duplicate-deduction protection, and separate attendance/payment states. Conversely, prepayment introduces payment failure handling. The relative effort depends on scope. “One room turning over cannot run a card for every name” also needs arrival-volume evidence: some arrivals already hold packs, and desk purchases can fund several future visits. Saying door payment “gives up the prepay a pack is for” conflates buying a pack at reception with selling individual classes.

Similarly, “a 10-minute timer mostly expires” and an email offer “will not clear in time” inside three hours (§2 and owner questions) are unsupported outcome predictions. Response rates, travel time, queue length, and offer duration are unknown. Email creates delay risk, but the document supplies no measurements. Automatic promotion being “reliable” the day before also depends on changed plans and reminder timing.

## Missing weight, and where Grok improves on my file

Grok mentions one credit across waitlists but underweights reservation versus consumption, studio cancellations, duplicate payments, renewal failures, and concurrent offers. An exclusive offer must hold capacity; “the spot stays open” (§2) leaves unclear whether a walk-in can take it. My file more clearly separates those states and flags expiry/acceptance races. Unlimited memberships also weaken any assumed per-class financial deterrent to no-shows.

Grok is stronger on concrete vendor exceptions. Momence’s [class FAQ](https://help.momence.com/en/articles/12028307-waitlist-faqs-classes) confirms that an unanswered round stops until another cancellation—an idle-seat risk my file missed. Mindbody’s [late-window automation guide](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/How-to-enable-the-Late-Cancellation-Window-Automation?language=en_US) supports Grok’s automatic-versus-broadcast distinction, with package and two-way-SMS conditions that deserve inclusion. Its questions about actual chat response times and routine walk-ups are particularly useful evidence requests. These improve the human’s decision inputs without resolving either open question.
